The Game of RuneVault 
 
In this project, you will implement RuneVault (RV), a single-player dungeon-crawler in the 
tradition of classic rogue-like games. You play as an Arcanist, a scholar of forbidden magic who 
has descended into the Cursed Vault beneath the Academy, seeking the five Shards of the 
Shattered Seal. Each Shard is locked on a different vault level and guarded by increasingly 
powerful Spectral Wardens. 
A game of RuneVault consists of a board 79 columns wide and 30 rows tall (5 rows are 
reserved for displaying status information). The Arcanist moves through five vault levels, 
dispelling wards, collecting rune fragments, and defeating spectral enemies until claiming the 
Shard on level 5 or being overwhelmed by the Vault's defences. RuneVault redraws the entire 
board after every command rather than updating in real time. 
 
Some Definitions 
• Arcanist (AC):  The player character, controlled by the player. Always shown as @ on 
the board. 
• Spectre:  A non-playable enemy character inhabiting the vault. All Spectres are hostile 
unless noted otherwise. 
• Rune Fragment (RF):  The primary currency of RuneVault. Collected by walking over it 
or defeating certain enemies. 
• Ward:  A magical lock on the descent to the next level. Only lifted when the Arcanist 
carries the Cipher Gem for that level.

• Vault Level:  A predefined configuration of 5 chambers connected by arcane corridors. 
The configuration is the same on every level. 
• Focus Points (FP):  The representation of the Arcanist's magical reserves, equivalent to 
HP. When FP reaches 0, the run ends. 
• Power (Pwr):  The representation of a character's offensive strength. 
• Ward Rating (WR):  The representation of a character's magical resistance, equivalent 
to Def. 
• Cell:  Either a vault wall, floor tile, archway, or corridor segment. 
• Spawned:  An enemy, fragment, or scroll has been placed on the board at the start of 
the level. 
• 1 Block Radius:  The 8 cells immediately adjacent to a character or item. 
• Shard: A Shard of the Shattered Seal, represented in play as a Shard Coffer (see Rune 
Fragments). Exactly one Shard is placed on every vault level, guarded by a Vault 
Anchor.

Spectres 
Spectres are the Arcanist's adversaries inside the vault. Unless noted otherwise, Spectres move 
one square randomly within the chamber in which they were spawned. The exception is Vault 
Anchors, who are stationary and always guard a Shard Coffer.

Lich:  A Lich can be attacked and defeated by the Arcanist. Attacking or slaying a Lich causes 
every Lich on subsequent levels to become hostile, attacking the Arcanist if it passes within a 
one-block radius. 
Vault Anchors:  A Vault Anchor always spawns within a one-block radius of its Shard Coffer. 
Whenever a Shard Coffer is placed on a level, a Vault Anchor is also placed to guard it. The 
Vault Anchor is only hostile when the Arcanist is adjacent to the Shard Coffer. 
Spectre drops:  Upon defeat, any Spectre that is not a Vault Anchor or Lich drops 1 Rune 
Fragment, immediately added to the Arcanist's total. 
Spectre movement: Spectres (except Vault Anchors) move randomly one floor tile at a time, 
provided the tile is unoccupied. A Spectre can never leave the chamber in which it was 
spawned. Spectres are moved row by row, left to right. Any particular Spectre is moved at most 
once per Arcanist action. 
Spectre attacks:  If the Arcanist is within a 1-block radius of a hostile Spectre, that 
Spectre attacks instead of moving. There is a 50% chance the attack misses.

Arcanist cannot carry scrolls; a scroll can only be used when the Arcanist is within a 1-block

scroll's type is remembered for the remainder of the run; other scroll instances remain unknown.

Major Items 
Two unique items appear across the run, one recurring and one unique. 
 
The Cipher Gem (C):  Appears once per level. At the start of each level, one Spectre is chosen 
uniformly at random to carry the Cipher Gem, excluding Vault Anchors. When that Spectre is 
defeated, the Gem drops on its tile and is picked up by walking over it. Picking up the Cipher 
Gem lifts the Ward on the descent to the next level and reveals the stairway (shown as \) on the 
display. 
 
The Aegis Cloak (G):  Appears only once per run, on a randomly selected level (2–4). It follows 
the same placement rules as a Shard Coffer, always guarded by a Vault Anchor and 
inaccessible until that Anchor is defeated. While the Arcanist carries the Aegis Cloak, all 
damage taken is halved (rounded up). 
 
Design Question 5: 
How could you design item generation so that Rune Fragments, Scrolls, and Major Items reuse as 
much code as possible? How would you structure your hierarchy to avoid duplicating the 'guarded 
placement' logic shared by the Shard Coffer and the Aegis Cloak? 
 
Vault Levels 
 
Each vault level consists of 5 chambers connected in the same fixed configuration (see Figure 
1). All five levels share this layout. The chambers are connected as follows: chamber 1 
connects to chambers 2 and 3; chamber 2 connects to chamber 4; chamber 3 connects to 
chamber 4; and chamber 4 connects to chamber 5. Every chamber is therefore reachable from 
every other chamber. A corridor always meets a chamber at an archway (+).The Ward on the 
descent is lifted only when the Arcanist carries the Cipher Gem for that level.

Spawning Rules 
Arcanist:  Spawns randomly on a floor tile in any chamber. The Arcanist must not spawn in the 
same chamber as the descent point (stairway). Chamber selection and tile selection within the 
chamber are uniformly random. 
Descent (stairway):  Shown only after the Arcanist picks up the Cipher Gem. Without the Gem, 
the stairway tile looks like a regular floor tile, but walking over it has no effect until the Ward is 
lifted. 
Scrolls:  10 scrolls spawned per level, each of the 6 types with equal probability (1/6). Any 
chamber has an equal probability of hosting a scroll; any floor tile within the selected chamber is 
equally likely. 
Rune Fragments:  Exactly one Shard Coffer (the level's Shard) is spawned per level, guarded 
by a Vault Anchor. The remaining 9 Rune Fragment piles are common shards (probability 5/7) 
or resonant shards (probability 2/7). Chambers and floor tiles within chambers are equally likely. 
Spectre Spawn Distribution: 
20 Spectres spawned per level. Every chamber is equally likely to spawn any particular Spectre. 
Spawn probabilities (excluding Vault Anchors): 
 
 
Specter Probability 
Wraith 2/9 
Banshee 3/18 
Shade 5/18 
Revenant 1/9 
Specter Lord 1/9 
Lich 1/9 
 
 
Generation order:  Arcanist location, stairway location, scrolls, rune fragments, spectres. This 
ordering allows for consistent evaluation of random generation. 
Multiple objects cannot occupy the same cell. No two objects may ever share a space, except 
rune fragments, which are collected when the Arcanist walks over them. Spectres and items 
may only spawn on floor tiles, never in archways, corridors, or on the stairway.

Combat 
 
All Spectres except Liches and Vault Anchors are hostile by default. If the Arcanist enters within 
a 1-block radius of a hostile Spectre, the Spectre attacks instead of moving. Vault Anchors are 
only hostile when the Arcanist is adjacent to their Shard Coffer. Spectres never pursue the 
Arcanist. 
 
Initiative:  The Arcanist always has initiative and attacks first. 
Spectre attacks:  Auto-attack when the Arcanist is within a 1 block radius. There is a 50% 
chance the attack misses. 
Arcanist attacks:  The Arcanist may attack any Spectre within a 1-block radius. The Arcanist 
never misses. 
 
Damage Formula 
 
Damage(Defender) = ceiling( (100 / (100 + WR(Defender))) × Pwr(Attacker) ) 
 
where Attacker is the attacking character, and Defender is the character being attacked. 
 
If a Spectre's attack hits, the Arcanist's FP is reduced by Damage(AC) as calculated above 
(halved if the Aegis Cloak is carried). 
 
 
Aegis Cloak effect:  If the Arcanist carries the Aegis Cloak, the damage they take is halved 
(rounded up): 
 
Damage(AC) = ceiling( Damage(Defender) / 2 ) 
 
If both the Warden's −1 reduction and the Aegis Cloak apply, resolve them in this order: subtract 
the Warden's 1, halve the result rounding up, then apply the minimum of 1. 
Damage(AC) = max(1, ceiling((Damage − 1) / 2)) 
Example: base damage 4 → max(1, ceiling(3 / 2)) = 2.

Command Interpreter 
 
Initially, the game will prompt the player to select an Arcanist class or quit. Play then continues 
until the Arcanist reaches the end of level 5, their FP reaches 0, the player restarts, or the player 
quits. If the run ends, the player is offered the option to play again. 
 
The following commands are supported: 
• no, so, ea, we, ne, nw, se, sw , Moves the Arcanist one cell in the specified cardinal or 
diagonal direction. 
• u direction, Uses the scroll in the specified direction (e.g., u no, u ea). The scroll must 
be within a 1-block radius. 
• a direction: Attacks the Spectre in the specified direction. The Spectre must be 
immediately adjacent. If there is no Spectre adjacent in the specified direction, the attack 
command has no effect, and the turn is not consumed 
• s, h, w, v , Selects the Arcanist's class at game start (Sage, Hexblade, Warden, 
Voidwalker). 
• r , Restarts the run. All stats, scrolls, and rune fragments are reset. A new class must be 
selected. 
• q , Quits the game. 
The board redraws after every command. 
 
Ending the Run and Scoring 
 
A run ends in one of the following ways: 
• The Arcanist reaches and descends the stairway on level 5 (win). 
• The Arcanist's FP reaches 0 (loss). 
• The player restarts the run. 
 
A score is generated only on a win. The score equals the total Rune Fragments collected, 
except that Sages receive a 50% bonus to their final score.

Grading & Submission 
 
Submission Requirements 
Your project will be evaluated through the following deliverables: 
• Code: Submitted on Marmoset. Make sure your program compiles and runs before 
submitting. 
• Demo: A live demonstration of your working program to the course staff. 
• Report: See the separate Phase 1 and Phase 2 Report Requirement documents. 
 
If Things Go Wrong 
If time is running short, here is the suggested order of priorities: 
1.  Reading and displaying a vault level with Spectres, scrolls, rune fragments, and descent 
point at specified locations. 
2.  A general-purpose Arcanist, Spectre enemies, and items (no special types yet). 
3.  Movement and interaction (combat, scroll use, fragment collection) for the generalized 
versions. 
4.  Different Arcanist classes and Spectre types. 
5.  Different scroll and rune fragment types. 
6.  Random level generation. 
 
Grading note:  Accomplishing the first three points should earn at least 50%, assuming good object -
oriented principles. A program that runs but is incomplete always receives a higher mark than one 
that attempts everything but does not compile. 
 
If Things Go Well (Bonus Features) 
If you complete the full specification, you may implement bonus features for extra credit. You 
must support play both with and without your bonus features; recompilation with different flags is 
not permitted. 
• Curses library: Use a curses/ncurses library for real-time WASD controls. 
• Inventory system: Allow the Arcanist to carry multiple scrolls; add new relic-type items. 
• Spectre intelligence: Give Spectres the ability to pursue the Arcanist across corridors. 
• Lich trading: Allow Liches to exchange rune fragments for scroll hints before hostilities 
are declared. 
• Prestige system: Add a class upgrade mechanic (e.g., Sage → Archmage) that unlocks 
after reaching level 3. 
• Additional classes, Spectres, or items that pose interesting OOP design challenges. 
 
Note:  To earn significant credit, enhancements must be algorithmically complex or solve an 
interesting OOP design problem. Trivial additions will not earn a significant fraction of available 
marks.

A Note on Random Generation 
This project requires pseudo-random number generation. See the provided shuffle.cc for an 
example of seeding and using a random number generator. The generator should be seeded 
once at startup. Runs started with the same seed will produce identical sequences of random 
events.
