Question 1:
Rune Vault: Lich
When an initially non-hostile Lich is attacked but survives, should that same Lich become permanently hostile and attack the current Arcanist when adjacent, or should only Liches generated on subsequent levels become hostile? The Spectre table suggests that the attacked Lich becomes hostile, while the detailed Lich rule specifically mentions Liches on subsequent levels.
In other words, if the Lich spawns non-hostile, and then the player attacks the Lich, moves away and then comes close to the Lich again, will the Lich still be hostile?

Answer 1:
The two statements are not in conflict. Treat Lich hostility as one global, run-scoped flag. The moment any Lich is attacked (survives or not), it flips permanently: that Lich, any other already-spawned Liches, and every future Lich all become hostile. "Subsequent levels" just calls out the forward propagation; it doesn't exclude the current level.
Short answer: attack, walk away, come back, still hostile. Only a restart (r) resets it. Implementation details are your call, as long as the end behaviour matches.

Follow-Up Question 1: Following up, does this mean if we attack a Lich, it should counterattack (as part of the same user action turn), or it does not attack in the same turn but is hostile every turn after?
Follow-Up Answer 1: Attack a non-hostile Lich, it survives, and if you're still within its 1-block radius (which you will be, since you just attacked it), it counterattacks immediately as part of resolving that turn.
The Arcanist always has initiative and attacks first". The Arcanist's attack resolves first, which is what flips the Lich's hostility flag. Spectre turn processing happens afterward, using the current state. Since the flag is already set by that point, and the Arcanist is adjacent (having just melee-attacked it), a surviving Lich attacks back in that same action rather than waiting for the next command.


Question 2:
Rune Vault Scrolls being "Remembered"
Can someone clarify what scrolls being "remembered" means from this section?
"Scrolls are the consumable items of RuneVault. All scrolls are shown as ? on the board. The Arcanist cannot carry scrolls; a scroll can only be used when the Arcanist is within a 1-block radius of it. The effect of a scroll is not known until it is first used in a session. After first use, that scroll's type is remembered for the remainder of the run; other scroll instances remain unknown."
Does this mean the scroll is only supposed to appear as a '?' on the board until a scroll of that type has been used previously? That's how I wanted to interpret it, but then we would need new chars to display the scrolls after their type has been revealed. Since the grid has a specific layout, I think it would be a bad idea to use the given abbreviations to do this, because they are more than 1 char long, which would make the display inconsistent.

Answer 2:
The board symbol doesn't change. The spec says "All scrolls are shown as ?" with no exception, so every scroll is always displayed as ?, identified or not. No new characters needed.
"Remembered" is about what the game knows, not what is drawn. Once a scroll type is used, its effect is known for the rest of the run. So if the Arcanist later uses another scroll of that same type, you can report its effect immediately in the status area instead of treating it as a fresh discovery. Unidentified types still get revealed only on first use. How exactly you word that status message is your call; just do not leak a scroll's type before it is used, and keep the board symbol as ? .

Question 8:
RuneVault blank rows
A bit of a silly question, but since the entire board is 30 rows, the status takes 5 rows, and the map from the diagram is 19 rows, does it matter where the remaining 30 - 5 - 19 = 6 blank rows go? I'm thinking of centering the map so 3 empty rows above it and 3 empty rows between the map and the status rows, just wanted to confirm whether there was anything specific we need

Answer 8:
It doesn't matter. The spec only fixes two things: the board is 79x30, and the bottom 5 rows are status. It says nothing about where padding goes above the map. Centring it, top-anchoring it, bottom-anchoring it against the status rows. Any of these is fine; it is just a display choice.

Follow-up:
Why is the board 79x30 when only the diagram only takes up 19 rows? Is 79 just a magic number?

Follow-up Answer:
Not arbitrary. 80 columns by roughly 24 to 25 rows was the classic terminal size that old roguelikes built their full screen around. 79 instead of 80 avoids a common terminal bug where printing exactly 80 characters then a newline causes an unwanted line wrap. The 30 rows break down as 25 for the map viewport (matching that classic terminal height) plus 5 for status.

So the 79x30 board describes the display window, not the level itself. The level (19 rows) is drawn inside that fixed viewport with blank space around it, which is also why padding placement doesn't matter, as answered in the blank rows thread.

Question 12:
RuneVault - VaultAnchor hostility
In the specification, this is what it mentions about Vault Anchors:
"Vault Anchors are only hostile when the Arcanist is adjacent to their Shard Coffer."

Consider this scenario:
@A*
The Arcanist is not adjacent to the Shard Coffer, so this would imply the Vault Anchor is not hostile. Then, the Arcanist repeatedly attacks the Vault Anchor until it dies, and the Vault Anchor is not hostile this entire time. Can someone please confirm we are truly meant to follow the specification here?

Also, consider this scenario:
A*@
The Arcanist is adjacent to the Shard Coffer, so the Vault Anchor is hostile. However, the Arcanist is not in a one-tile radius of the Vault Anchor itself. Due to this statement: "Spectre attacks: If the Arcanist is within a 1-block radius of a hostile Spectre, that Spectre attacks instead of moving.", I would assume the Vault Anchor is not a special case, i.e., it still cannot attack until the Arcanist moves into a 1-tile radius of the Vault Anchor itself. Can someone confirm this? Thank you!

Answer 12:
Confirmed on both counts => follow the spec literally here.

Scenario 1 (@A*): Hostility is gated only on adjacency to the Coffer, not to the Anchor itself. If the Arcanist is standing next to the Anchor but not the Coffer, the Anchor stays non-hostile and never retaliates, even while being attacked to death. That is intentional, if a little strange thematically; it is an exploitable "safe kill" the spec allows.

Scenario 2 (A*@): Right again. Coffer-adjacency only toggles whether the Anchor is hostile; it doesn't change the attack trigger. Attacking still follows the general rule: a hostile Spectre only attacks once the Arcanist is within a 1-block radius of it specifically. So a hostile Anchor sitting 2 tiles away from the Arcanist (because the Coffer is between them) won't attack until the Arcanist closes the distance to the Anchor itself.

Hostility (from Coffer adjacency) and attack range (adjacency to the Anchor) are two separate checks, not one.


Question 13:
RuneVault: Where does the Lich Cache Drop
After I kill the Lich where does the Lich Cache Drop?

Answer 13:
On the Lich's own tile. The generic "Spectre drops 1 Rune Fragment, immediately added to the total" rule explicitly excludes Liches, and the fragment table separately says the Lich cache is "dropped" rather than auto-collected, same pattern as the Cipher Gem, which "drops on its tile" when its carrier is defeated. So when the Lich dies, the cache appears as a physical item on the tile it was standing on, and the Arcanist has to walk over it to collect it, rather than getting it credited instantly.


Question 14:
RuneVault - what counts as "pursuing"?
Under the Combat section, we have this statement:
"Spectres never pursue the Arcanist."

First of all, was this meant to be a general statement applying to all Spectres, not just ones that just conducted combat with the Arcanist? I ask because if a Spectre attacks an Arcanist, it does not move that turn, so this rule would be redundant if only considered under the Combat section.

Following up, if it is meant to apply to all Spectres, what counts as "pursuing"? Is it as simple as a Spectre cannot move onto the tile the Arcanist just left, or a Spectre cannot move in the direction the Arcanist just moved? I could see many scenarios where the user would feel they are being "pursued" that is not covered by either of the two things I have just said, so some clarification would be great, thanks!


Answer 14:
Yes, it is a general statement, and it is not redundant with the adjacency rule. "Attacks instead of moving when adjacent" only covers the 1-block-radius case. "Never pursue" covers every other turn: it says the random movement described earlier (move one floor tile at a time, chosen randomly) must never be biased toward the Arcanist's position at any distance.

This means: do not write any logic that looks at the Arcanist's location when choosing a Spectre's move. If movement is a genuine uniform-random pick among legal adjacent floor tiles, you satisfy "never pursue" automatically, you do not need extra checks to block a Spectre from stepping onto the tile the Arcanist just left or moving in the same direction the Arcanist did. Those can happen by pure chance under random movement and are fine. "Pursue" means deliberately tracking or chasing (e.g., pathfinding toward the Arcanist), not incidental proximity from randomness.
